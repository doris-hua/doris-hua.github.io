I was sitting in my biology class on evolution when this slide was presented:

This slide presented the traditional thinking behind sexual selection, one of the main forces behind evolution. All of us were very familiar with these rules, though this time I was troubled by their simplified presentation. Worse, I noticed how similar they were to the ideology of an online community that has been increasingly prevalent in internet discourse. A group of people who live by these rules (which are much more nuanced in reality). A subculture online called ‘incels’.

Incels are an online community of predominantly heterosexual men who define themselves as “involuntary celibates”: unable to form or access sexual relationships with women. As a result, these men have an extremely hateful relationship with themselves and with women. Over the past decade, misogynistic and violent attacks linked to members of this community have increasingly brought their extreme ideology under examination.

Incels believe they are victims of a hidden social and biological reality: women are evolutionarily trained to prefer certain traits (e.g., money, good looks), which incels are socially and genetically unable to obtain.

Before their ban from the site in the late 2010s, incels existed largely on Reddit on forums such as r/incels and r/braincels. Today, their community hosts their own forum, most of which requires registration to view. However, they also host a public Wikipedia page, where, in addition to presenting their own theories, they have compiled a master list of hundreds of peer-reviewed research papers that support their beliefs. These papers are mainly from the field of evolutionary psychology: regarding topics in sex, dating, and female mate preferences.

As a biology student, I was curious and concerned. How much of incel culture is based on science?

To pursue this question, I used an archived dataset of 100,000 incel messages from the Reddit subreddit r/incel from 2017. With the help of a computer, I collected all the messages, removed duplicates and punctuation, separated the words, and started counting.

With these methods, we can determine the most commonly used words on the incel subreddit.
By comparing these words to the most popular subreddits at the time, we can also determine the words that are used more by incels.

You can see that incels have a very specialized language, many of these words being used to describe women or members of their community who belong to various minorities.
Searching for biological terms in this way can give us an idea of how often concepts like mating or genes are being used.


The word "evolutionary", for example, appears 3x more frequently on the incel subreddits.

Further, to understand how these terms were being used, I used a common text analysis tool to quantify how negative and positive each message was.

This gives us the overall “sentiment” of the Incel subreddit, rated on a scale of –1 (most negative) to 1 (most positive).
Surprisingly, the overall sentiment across the incel subreddit and the popular subreddits was very similar.
However, looking at just the messages containing biological terms tells a different story. The messages seem to skew more sentimental towards both sides.
Compared to the messages containing incel words, it seems incels use biological terms more positively than their specialized jargon.

Using these text analysis tools, I was able to adopt a “distant reading” approach, which comes with the benefit of being able to analyze thousands of messages at once. At the same time, it came with the challenge of defining a good question within the models being used and producing insights that still recognized textual nuance. There is still definitely room to improve, particularly with the sentiment analysis and its definition of “positive” and “negative”.

This exploration does show that the relationship between incels and biological concepts is real and manifests in several ways. Incels list specific scientific papers on their Wikipedia to objectively prove their worldview, and at the same time, use biological terms in a non-objective manner elsewhere online.

Understanding this extreme application of biology can give us a better understanding of how science can be misinterpreted, lending some insight into how the concepts on my biology slides evolved outside of the classroom.
